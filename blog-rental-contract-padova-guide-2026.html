Quick answer

In Padua, most residential leases are 4+4 free rent or 3+2 agreed rent, registered with Agenzia delle Entrate. Deposits, notice and tax treatment differ. This English guide explains choices for expats — based on our Italian contract guide, rewritten for international readers.

Signing without understanding contract type is the main source of expat disputes. This article explains structures, not personalised tax advice — consult a commercialista for cedolare secca or IMU interactions.

Main contract types

4+4 (libero): four years + renewal four years; rent negotiated freely within market. 3+2 (concordato): rent within agreed tables — tax benefits. Transitorio: 1–18 months for documented temporary reasons (transfer, study, building works). Studenti fuori sede: special agreed schemes where applicable.

Registration (registrazione)

Lease must be registered with Agenzia delle Entrate within deadlines; late registration triggers penalties. Agency can file electronically. Keep receipt for residence registration (anagrafe) if you need certificato di residenza.

Deposit (deposito cauzionale) vs advance (caparra)

Deposit secures damages — typically up to three months, returned minus legitimate deductions. Caparra confirmatoria binds parties if deal fails — legal effects differ. Never pay cash without receipt.

Tax notes (high level)

Landlords may choose cedolare secca on eligible leases instead of IRPEF on rent. Tenants: rent paid is not deductible for standard employees. IMU generally on owner. Verify with professional.

Avoiding disputes

  • Attach APE, inventory, meter readings to contract annex.
  • Clarify who maintains boiler and appliances.
  • Condominium rules on pets and quiet hours.
  • Early termination clauses — read before signing.
