INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL · PROPERTY PLANNING

The real ownership budget: beyond the asking price

Build a property budget that separates purchase costs, annual ownership, finance, works and a downside scenario. No fixed tax or yield assumptions.

Righetto International · Sources checked 24 November 2026 · General property information

Separate the price from the capital required

An asking price is one part of the decision. Build a written budget with separate lines for the agreed price, transaction taxes, notarial and agency fees, technical advice, finance costs and any work before occupation. Ask who supplies each estimate, what it includes and when it becomes payable. Keep estimates marked as estimates until the relevant professional or supplier confirms them.

Purchase tax is not one universal percentage

The Italian Revenue Agency explains that tax treatment depends on the seller and on whether the buyer qualifies for first-home benefits. A transaction subject to VAT differs from one subject to proportional registration tax. The relevant tax base also matters. Ask the notary or tax adviser for a calculation for the actual property and buyer. Do not transfer a percentage from a general guide straight onto the asking price.

Plan for the period after completion

Create a second budget for ownership: building or condominium charges, utilities, insurance, maintenance, any management service and applicable taxes. Ask for the available building accounts and records, including planned work where relevant. A low purchase price does not answer what the property costs to run. If works are proposed, obtain the necessary professional assessment and written estimates before assuming their cost or feasibility.

If you expect rent, test the assumptions

A gross rent projection is not net income. Consider periods without a tenant, management, repairs, tax, finance and costs of changing occupants. Confirm that the intended letting activity is permitted and what obligations apply. Compare a base case with a less favourable case, such as lower income or a longer vacancy. No standard return is promised for Padova, Veneto or a property category.

Keep a decision sheet

For each candidate, record the source and date of every cost, the uncertainties still open and the next check needed. Compare properties on the same basis. Include a reserve you choose for unexpected costs, rather than spending every available euro at purchase.
