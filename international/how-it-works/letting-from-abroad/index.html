Letting a property while living abroad: questions to settle

Prepare a clear letting brief for a Padova property: intended use, responsibilities, costs, records and communication when the owner is abroad.

Righetto International · Sources checked 24 November 2026 · General property information

Define the letting you actually intend

Residential, student, temporary and tourist uses raise different questions. Start with the property, intended occupiers and expected pattern of use, then ask which agreement and obligations fit the case. Living abroad does not itself determine the right tenancy or tax treatment. Do not rely on a short online summary to choose a contract or assume a net return.

Agree who is responsible for each task

Righetto’s local team assigns residential and student lettings to Linda; Gino handles commercial lettings. The published service descriptions cover lettings and property management, but the scope for your instruction needs agreement. Ask who handles occupant communication, payments, maintenance coordination, documents and deadlines, and what requires your decision. A management label alone does not define every responsibility.

Build a complete cost and risk picture

Compare the expected rent with property charges, management fees, maintenance, tax, vacancy and any finance costs. Confirm how tenant checks and the deposit will be handled, without assuming they eliminate risk. Ask how late payment, urgent repairs and disputes would be communicated and which professional would advise on them. No eviction timescale or occupancy level is guaranteed.

Treat tourist letting as a separate check

Do not assume a property can be used for tourist or short-term accommodation because it can be let residentially. Ask the appropriate advisers and authorities to check the intended activity against current national, regional and municipal requirements and relevant building rules. Identify the full operating tasks and costs before depending on projected income.

Start with a safe first enquiry

Describe the locality, property type, current use, occupancy position and the outcome you want. Mention that you live abroad and your preferred language and contact method. Do not send identity documents, bank details or an existing tenant’s personal records in the first message. Agree an appropriate process for any documents needed later. This article is a planning checklist, not a tenancy, tax or legal opinion.
