INVESTMENT

Start with the assumptions, not the headline return.

An asking price and an estimated rent do not tell you whether an investment fits your plans. Set out the purpose, the cash required and the risks before comparing properties.

INVESTMENTItalia.A planning framework, not a completed project

How should you assess a property investment idea?

Compare the total capital required with the intended use, ownership costs and realistic income assumptions. Test what happens if the property is vacant, work costs more or resale takes longer. This is a planning framework, not a yield forecast, valuation or investment recommendation.

THE PAGE AT A GLANCE

Questions worth settling early.

A practical checklist for your discussion, not a fixed promise about the property or outcome.

01

Choose the purpose

Is the property for personal use, a long-term letting, business occupation or a future sale? Different uses call for different checks. Do not build an investment plan around a use that has not been assessed for the individual property.

02

Separate price from total capital

Build a budget that distinguishes the purchase price from transaction costs, professional fees, financing, any work and an initial reserve. Ask the notary and tax adviser to confirm the tax treatment for your situation.

03

Test the income assumptions

For a letting scenario, record the proposed rent and the evidence behind it. Then allow for vacancy, management, maintenance and other ownership costs. A gross rental estimate is not money available to spend.

04

Ask what could change

Consider a period without rent, unexpected work, financing changes and a slower resale. These are planning scenarios, not predictions. A decision should remain understandable even when an optimistic assumption is removed.

05

Review the property itself

Establish what documentation and technical inspections are needed before committing. Condition, permitted use and any proposed alterations should be checked for the specific property, not inferred from a listing.

06

Plan ownership and exit

Discuss who will manage the property, how decisions will be made and what would trigger a sale. If you live abroad, clarify the tax and administrative implications with advisers in the relevant jurisdictions.
