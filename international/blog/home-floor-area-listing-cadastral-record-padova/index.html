Home square metres: which figure should you use in Padua?

The listing shows one floor area, the cadastral extract (visura catastale) shows another, and measuring the rooms gives you a third. Before concluding that there is a mistake, check that the figures describe the same thing. For sellers in Padua and Limena, clarifying this point helps present the home without misunderstandings and compare prices using a readable criterion.

In brief

There is no figure to pick simply because it is the highest. Every surface area must come with its name, its source and its calculation criterion. The cadastral surface follows its own rules; a floor area used in a listing must be explained; the internal measurement of the rooms is not automatically equivalent to the other two.

How Righetto can help

Organise the available documents, clarify the origin of each area figure and prepare the questions before presenting the home. Technical checks remain the responsibility of a qualified professional.

Why can the listing and the visura show different square metres?

Two different figures do not, on their own, prove either a mistake or a non-conformity. They may refer to different perimeters: internal rooms only, walls included, ancillary areas counted in part, or an entire cadastral unit. The first check is not subtracting one total from the other, but reading the description of what each total includes.

The Agenzia delle Entrate guide to the visura catastale distinguishes the total surface and, for group A categories, the total surface excluding uncovered areas. Even within the same document, then, two surface areas with different meanings can appear. Reading only the number and skipping the wording removes the information needed to interpret it.

For the owner the useful question is simple: am I comparing the same object using the same criterion? A listing that also includes an ancillary space cannot be compared directly with the floor measurement of the apartment. First the components are separated, then you assess whether the difference can be explained.

Sources: Agenzia delle Entrate, La visura catastale, novembre 2025

What does cadastral surface mean?

The cadastral surface is a figure determined under regulatory criteria, not the simple sum of the space you can walk on. The visura guide refers to DPR 138/1998. In Annex C the calculation takes into account walls and the various components of the property according to specific rules. It is therefore not correct to treat it as a net internal measurement.

For dwellings, the wording on the exclusion of uncovered areas concerns the contribution of balconies, terraces and uncovered ancillary and accessory areas indicated by the guide. It does not mean that the second total is automatically the walkable floor area. Here too, the full name of the entry matters more than the habit of how it is called.

This guide does not suggest redoing the cadastral calculation by hand. If a figure does not add up, you need to understand which document was used, which unit it refers to and whether a technical check is needed. A photo of the visura cropped to the number alone is not enough: it can hide the wording and the reference that explain it.

Sources: Agenzia delle Entrate, La visura catastale, novembre 2025

Which floor area should be clarified before writing the listing?

Before the listing, you need to clarify what is meant by the declared floor area. If a commercial surface is used, ask for the calculation breakdown: spaces included, starting surfaces, any weightings and the treatment of ancillary areas. The aim is not to find a percentage to apply to everything, but to make the final figure traceable.

In listing language, internal, usable or walkable surface may be used without sufficient explanation. To avoid misunderstandings, it is better to describe the object measured and the method, rather than rely on the label alone. Do not automatically convert cadastral square metres into internal square metres with a fixed reduction: this page does not propose a universal formula.

The owner of an apartment in Limena with a terrace and a garage can prepare a separate list of the three elements. This is an organisational example, not a case followed by the agency. It does not give the terrace or the garage the same weight as the dwelling and does not establish their value. It serves to bring out the questions before the figure goes online.

Sources: DPR 23 marzo 1998 n.138, Allegato C, testo ospitato da Agenzia delle Entrate

How do you avoid counting the balcony and garage twice?

Ancillary areas must be identified and treated consistently in the calculation and in the presentation of the price. If an element already contributes to the total used, adding it again without explanation can make the comparison misleading. The practical check is to note where each component appears, without assuming that all listings adopt the same criterion.

In the file, prepare separate lines for the dwelling, balcony or terrace, cellar, garage and outdoor area, where present. Next to each line, state the available document and how it enters the total. If an item is still to be verified, write exactly that: do not replace the missing information with a percentage chosen out of habit.

Annex C of DPR 138/1998 provides detailed cadastral rules for accessories and ancillary areas. Transferring an isolated coefficient from those rules to any listing would be a shortcut. The cadastral figure and the commercial presentation have different functions: if technical criteria are cited, you must say which ones and apply them in their context.

Sources: DPR 23 marzo 1998 n.138, Allegato C, testo ospitato da Agenzia delle Entrate

Why do square metres change the price comparison?

The price per square metre depends on the number of square metres used as the divisor. With the total price unchanged, dividing by a different surface produces a different value. Before comparing two homes you therefore need to align the surfaces, clarify what the price includes and consider the features and condition of the properties.

The same applies when consulting OMI quotations. The Agenzia delle Entrate summary guide distinguishes the surface reference in the data and reminds users that the quotations give broad indications only. They do not replace the appraisal of the individual property. It is not enough to multiply a figure found online by the most favourable number in your own documents.

For Padua and its hinterland, this page does not publish average prices, local ranges or adjustments. Without a verified selection of area, period, type and surface, a figure would add apparent precision. The subject here is narrower: preparing a clear basis so that the subsequent market comparison does not start from different quantities.

Sources: Agenzia delle Entrate, Guida sintetica per l'utilizzo delle quotazioni OMI

What check should you make if the numbers still do not add up?

If the definitions are clear but an unexplained difference remains, gather the documents and ask for a check before correcting the listing. A value in the visura, a floor plan and the actual state of the premises answer related but different questions. The explanation of a floor area does not, on its own, certify the property's regularity.

Avoid two opposite conclusions: declaring everything correct because the number appears in a document, or announcing a non-conformity based only on the gap between totals. The comparison must be reconstructed. If a survey or a compliance assessment is needed, that step belongs to the competent technician, not to an editorial correction of the description.

When preparing a sale it is also worth distinguishing roles. The agency helps organise information, presentation and market comparison; technical checks and legal matters require the competent professionals. The Notariato recommends involving the notary from the early stages of a property purchase, before making any commitments.

Sources: Consiglio Nazionale del Notariato, La compravendita

What should you bring to the first meeting with the agency?

Bring the information you have, with its origin, and a list of the differences to clarify. An incomplete but readable file is more useful than a total reconstructed without method. You do not need to publish personal documents: the listing and the file to share with professionals have different recipients and levels of detail.

In the first meeting, separate what you know from what must be verified: the figure in the previous listing, the surfaces shown in the visura, the ancillary areas included, any measurements already carried out. If a document is missing, note it. Do not make names, full cadastral identifiers or private details public just to ask for a simple clarification about the floor area.

FAQ

Is the cadastral surface the same as the walkable surface?

No, it should not be considered equivalent automatically. The cadastral surface follows the criteria of DPR 138/1998 and includes components that do not coincide with the interior floor alone. Before comparing the numbers, always check the full name of the entry, the unit it refers to and the criterion used for the other measurement.

Do two different surface areas prove a mistake in the listing?

Not on their own. They may describe different components or criteria. To understand, you need the source of each figure and a breakdown of what is included. If the difference remains unexplained, ask for a check before changing the text or presenting the number as certain.

Can I derive the internal square metres by deducting a fixed percentage?

This guide does not propose a fixed conversion. Walls, layout and ancillary areas make it improper to treat all homes the same way. You need to start from the definition of the surface sought and from suitable documents or surveys, not choose a reduction because it was used in another listing.

Can I multiply the visura floor area by an OMI quotation?

It is not a sufficient method to estimate an individual home. First you need to check that the surface is consistent with the quotation's reference. Then there remain area, period, type and the property's features. The Agenzia delle Entrate states that OMI quotations are an aid and do not replace a specific appraisal.

Does a correct floor area also certify the regularity of the home?

No. Clarifying the number of square metres and compliance checks are not the same thing. If doubts arise about the documents or the state of the premises, the competent technician must be involved. A broader check cannot be closed by correcting only the listing description.
