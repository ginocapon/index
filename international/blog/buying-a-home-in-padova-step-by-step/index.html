Buying a home in Padova: step-by-step guide

Gino Capon · Adapted from the Italian article · Editorial revision 25 November 2026

Eight steps from your budget to the keys, with the documents to request and the questions to take to your bank, technician and notary.

In short

Start with your total budget and financing. Compare properties, check the documents and agree the conditions before signing. An accepted offer can already have legal consequences: involve your advisers early. This guide makes no market forecast and gives no standard discount, mortgage rate or deposit percentage.

1. Set the total budget

Separate the purchase price from taxes, professional fees, agency fees, bank costs, possible works and a reserve for unexpected costs. Request written estimates for your transaction rather than applying a generic percentage to the price.

Ask the notary which taxes apply to the seller, the property and any first-home relief you may qualify for. Do not assume that every sale by a business has the same VAT treatment, or that a relief applies without checking its conditions.

2. Discuss financing before committing

If you need a mortgage, ask the bank to assess your position and explain the documents, valuation and conditions for approval. A preliminary indication is not a guarantee that a particular property will be financed or that the money will be available by a chosen date.

Coordinate the offer, financing deadline and intended deed date. Ask your adviser to draft any financing condition in terms that fit your case, including the amount, deadline and what happens if the condition is not met.

3. Compare the property, not a market-wide promise

Use your location, space, accessibility and running-cost needs to build a shortlist in Padova and the surrounding towns. Check current availability directly. Ask what supports the asking price and compare properties with similar characteristics.

A listing is a starting point, not proof of planning compliance, title or condition. Do not treat an online image, an estimated price per square metre or an unverified market statistic as a substitute for the documents.

4. View with a checklist

Look at light, noise, access, damp, windows, heating and the visible condition of the building. If something is unclear, ask a qualified technician. The appearance or direction of a crack alone cannot establish whether it is harmless.

For an apartment, request the condominium rules, recent meeting minutes, accounts, payment statement and details of approved works. Ask your notary to explain outstanding costs and liability before completion.

5. Check documents before signing

The Agenzia delle Entrate recommends checking cadastral and mortgage information and buying from the legitimate owner. Mortgage-register searches help identify ownership and registered mortgages or other entries. Important checks are also carried out by the notary preparing the deed.

Ask your technician to compare the actual property with the planning documents and cadastral floor plan, and identify any discrepancies and their consequences. Ask for the energy certificate and available system documents. Agree what must be supplied or addressed, by whom and by when.

6. Understand the written offer

Before signing, review the property identification, price, payment terms, acceptance deadline, any commitment to keep the offer open, conditions and intended deed date. Do not assume you can withdraw without consequences.

An accepted offer may already amount to a preliminary contract, depending on its terms. The Agenzia delle Entrate explains that an acceptance sufficient to conclude a preliminary contract can also require registration. Ask the notary to assess the actual wording.

7. Register the preliminary contract

A preliminary contract creates reciprocal obligations to enter into the final sale; it does not itself transfer ownership. The current Revenue Agency guidance requires registration within 30 days of signing. If it is a notarial act, the notary handles registration within that time.

Registration normally involves a fixed registration tax of 200 euro and stamp duty. For preliminary contracts entered into from 1 January 2025, sums paid as a confirmatory deposit or as non-VAT advances are subject to 0.5% registration tax, or the lower tax applicable to the final contract. VAT advances have different treatment. Have the notary calculate the amount for your case.

The proportional tax is credited against the main registration tax due on the final contract. Do not treat every fixed cost as a refundable advance. Ask the notary whether transcription of the preliminary contract in the property registers is appropriate: it provides protection against certain later acts by the seller and requires notarial involvement.

8. Prepare the deed and handover

Agree the final document list, payment method, bank arrangements and handover terms with the notary and the other parties. Confirm how outstanding works, occupants, furniture, condominium costs and keys will be handled.

After completion, check the steps for utilities, local taxes and any conditions or deadlines attached to a tax relief. Ask for advice specific to your residence and transaction rather than relying on a generic timeline.

Questions buyers ask

How much cash will I need?

It depends on the agreed price, the financing actually approved, taxes, fees, works and reserves. Build a written budget from current quotations. This guide does not give a market-wide mortgage rate or a standard extra-cost percentage.

How long will it take?

Financing, document checks, negotiations and the parties' circumstances determine the timetable. Agree realistic dates after checking those dependencies, not on the assumption that every sale takes the same number of months.

Can I skip the preliminary contract?

Ask the notary which steps fit your case. Remember that an accepted offer may already create preliminary obligations and registration requirements even if a later document has not yet been signed.

General information, not advice for an individual legal, tax or financial decision. Adapted from the Italian original with editorial corrections; ask your qualified advisers to check the documents and current rules for your case.
