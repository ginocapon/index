INTERNATIONAL BLOG · BUYING

Condominium fees when buying in Padova

Check the building’s costs before you make an offer: rules, meeting minutes, the millesimi table and the seller’s payment record.

Righetto International · Translated from the Italian article of 29 August 2026 · General property information, not legal advice

In short

Condominium costs when buying a home in Padova should be checked before the offer: the building rules, the minutes of the owners’ meetings, the millesimi table and the seller’s payment record. Source: Italian Civil Code, articles 1117 to 1139. This article is about due diligence at the buying stage, not about running costs after completion.

What condominium costs are and why they matter

Buying an apartment in a condominium means joining a body that owns common parts (stairs, façades, lifts, shared systems) whose costs are split among the owners. The monthly or quarterly charges add to the total cost of the home, often as much as the price difference between two similar listings, or more.

At the buying stage the aim is not to predict next year’s charge to the cent, since it changes every year. The aim is to check that there are no surprises: extraordinary works already approved, unpaid charges by the seller, active disputes or restrictive building rules.

Civil Code: common parts (article 1117 onwards)

Article 1117 lists the common parts of a building: land, foundations, main walls, stairs, lifts and similar. They are owned jointly and maintenance costs are shared. Article 1118 separates the exclusive property of each unit from the common parts, which is essential to understand what you pay a share of.

Articles 1135 and 1136 concern the owners’ meeting and its decisions. Article 1139 instead refers to the general rules on co-ownership where the condominium provisions do not address a matter.

Millesimi and how costs are shared (article 1123)

Article 1123 says costs to maintain the common parts are shared in proportion to the value of each unit, unless the building rules say otherwise. The millesimi table shows each unit’s share out of one thousand.

An upper-floor apartment with a lift and a terrace can have more millesimi than a ground-floor one: same purchase price, different building costs. Always ask for the updated table and compare it with the cadastral floor plan.

Documents to ask for before the offer

  • Building rules: use of common parts, pets, letting, changes inside the unit.
  • Minutes of the latest meetings (at least 12 months): works, disputes, appointment of the administrator.
  • Final and budget accounts: expected ordinary and extraordinary costs.
  • Millesimi table: how the shares are split.
  • Administrator’s statement of the seller’s payments.

Ordinary and extraordinary costs

Ordinary costs cover day-to-day running: stair cleaning, lift, shared lighting, administrator. Extraordinary costs come from decisions of the meeting: façade, roof, energy upgrades, systems.

Approved extraordinary works can create substantial costs. Have the notary establish the allocation between buyer and seller, considering the date of the decision, the transfer and their agreement. Do not assume all future instalments automatically belong to the buyer.

Meeting decisions and risks for the buyer

The owners’ meeting decides on costs, works and internal rules. Decisions on structural work, accessibility or shared systems can mean significant charges split by millesimi.

Disputes mentioned in the minutes should be reviewed with a professional. Check the applicable challenge procedure and time limits for each decision.

Payment record and the seller’s statement

Obtain the administrator’s statement of payments and approved works before completion. Agree how any outstanding amounts will be dealt with; do not rely on an assumed processing time.

Under article 63 of the Civil Code implementing provisions, the incoming owner is jointly liable with the previous owner for contributions relating to the current and previous condominium accounting year. Ask the administrator and notary to establish the position before completion.

Central Padova and Limena

In Padova, older buildings in the semi-centre and districts such as Arcella and Portello often have older lifts and protected façades, so extraordinary works are more frequent. In Limena, more recent residential buildings may have lower ordinary costs, but still check the decisions on insulation or shared solar panels.

Checklist before you make an offer

  • Building rules and millesimi table requested.
  • Minutes of the last 12 to 24 months read: look for “extraordinary”, “façade”, “roof”.
  • Charges stated in the listing compared with the final accounts.
  • No seller arrears (administrator’s statement).
  • Impact of approved works not yet paid estimated in your future budget.
  • Estimated condominium cost included in the mortgage payment plus fixed costs.

Include the costs in your total budget

A bank looks at the ratio of payment to income. Fixed condominium charges raise the real monthly outgoings even when they are not always part of the mortgage calculation. For a first home in Padova, simulate: mortgage payment plus condominium plus utilities plus ordinary maintenance. For a specific property, use comparables, a visit and the documents. Never rely on invented market figures.

Frequently asked questions

When should I check the condominium costs?

Before the offer or deposit: rules, minutes and the seller’s payment record.

What should I ask the administrator?

The latest minutes, the accounts, the millesimi table and the statement of the seller’s payments.

Are approved extraordinary works a risk?

Approved works are a risk to check. Ask the notary who bears each cost and whether joint liability to the condominium applies.

What are millesimi?

The shares used to split costs (article 1123 of the Civil Code). The table is part of the building documents.

Does this apply in Limena too?

Yes. The same Civil Code rules apply; check the decisions even in recent buildings.
