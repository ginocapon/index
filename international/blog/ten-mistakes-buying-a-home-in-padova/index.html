Ten mistakes to avoid when buying a home in Padova

Ten practical checks for a home purchase in the Padova area, and a sequence to reduce avoidable risks.

G Translated from the Italian article by Gino Capon · Italian original 3 June 2026 · 6 min read

In short

The costly mistakes are signing offers without a mortgage pre-approval, skipping a second viewing or a technical check on older buildings, ignoring the seller’s condominium debts and approved works, and underestimating timing (mortgage approval, deed, conditions precedent).

Who this article is for

Buyers in the Padova area making their first or second purchase who want to avoid mistakes that recur in real negotiations.

What it is not

  • It is a practical checklist, not a statistical survey of transactions.
  • It does not cover every legal case.
  • It does not replace legal due diligence in complex cases.

Why a list of mistakes

This guide focuses on practical risks: deposits, financing, approved condominium works and document discrepancies. Ask your bank, technician and notary to assess the individual transaction.

Mistakes 1 to 3: credit and how you see the property

A bank assessment helps establish a realistic budget. Visit at different times and ask a technician to assess the property and its systems when needed.

Mistakes 4 to 6: condominium and contract

Ask for the administrator’s payment statement and the approved works. If you need financing, ask a professional to draft an appropriate mortgage condition before you sign.

Mistakes 7 to 10: documents, time, market

Check the cadastral floor plan and planning compliance. Align the deed calendar with the bank. Compare the price with OMI values and with sold prices, not only with aspirational listings. An energy class G home in areas where comparables are class D means a discount or works.

Padova in 2026

Compare the specific property, its documents, financing terms and recent local comparables. Do not infer a market-wide forecast from a single listing.

Bonus mistake: undervaluing useful mediation

Refusing professional support to “save the commission” can mean losing checks on title and price negotiation.

A sequence that avoids the mistakes

  • Pre-approval and a total budget (purchase + works + taxes).
  • Selection of micro-areas and comparables.
  • Repeated viewings with a technical checklist.
  • An offer with the right conditions precedent.
  • Document due diligence before the deed.
