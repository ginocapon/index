The taxes paid at the deed when buying a first home in Padova

Which tax questions to settle with the notary before buying, including the difference between a private sale and a VAT transaction.

Gino Capon · Adapted from the Italian article · Editorial revision 25 November 2026

In short

The tax treatment depends on the seller, the property, the transaction and any relief for which the buyer qualifies. Do not assume that every company sale is subject to VAT, or that a first purchase automatically qualifies for every first-home benefit. Ask the notary to identify the applicable regime in writing.

Ask for a transaction-specific tax estimate

The tax treatment depends on the seller, the property, the transaction and any relief for which the buyer qualifies. Do not assume that every company sale is subject to VAT, or that a first purchase automatically qualifies for every first-home benefit. Ask the notary to identify the applicable regime in writing.

Separate the different charges

Request a line-by-line estimate covering registration tax or VAT where applicable, mortgage and cadastral taxes, stamp and registration costs, the notary’s fee and any mortgage deed costs. VAT does not mean there are no registration-related fixed charges. Ask which amounts are taxes and which are professional fees.

Check relief before signing

Ask which declarations, property categories, residence conditions and existing ownership interests affect eligibility. Do not treat the historical under-36 purchase-tax relief as a general benefit for a new deed in 2026. The Revenue Agency’s guidance describes time-limited arrangements; a mortgage guarantee scheme is a separate question.

Tax base is not always the asking price

Ask whether the price-value rules or another calculation applies to this transaction. A cadastral figure, the agreed price and the taxable base are not interchangeable. Let the notary check the property’s records and explain the calculation instead of estimating from a generic online table.

Keep the budget beyond completion

Purchase charges are only part of ownership. Ask separately about IMU if due, TARI, condominium, utilities, insurance and upkeep. Keep the deed, declarations, tax calculations and payment receipts, and ask which later changes or deadlines could affect a relief.
