Checks before the preliminary contract in Padova

An ordered list of document, technical, mortgage and condominium checks to make before you sign the “compromesso”.

G Translated from the Italian article by Gino Capon · Italian original 3 June 2026 · 6 min read

In short

Before signing the preliminary contract check four things: documents (energy certificate, floor plan, searches, system certificates), structure and systems (damp, electrical and plumbing compliance), the mortgage (condition precedent, valuation, approval expiry) and the condominium (no debts, rules, extraordinary works).

Who this article is for

Buyers and sellers in the Padova area who must sign a preliminary contract and want an ordered list of technical and document checks.

What it is not

  • It is not a model contract that replaces the notary.
  • It does not cover sales involving companies and complex trusts.
  • It does not guarantee a mortgage approval.

Why check before the preliminary contract

The preliminary contract (“compromesso”) binds the parties on price, deposit and timing. After signing, leaving costs penalties. Technical checklists have to be adapted to the Padova context: condominiums often with extraordinary works, a historic centre with restrictions, and a surrounding belt with a mix of new and second-hand homes.

Structure and systems

A technical viewing can cover damp, heating, the electrical system, plumbing, windows and sound insulation. Ask a qualified technician which checks and certificates apply to this property.

Mortgage: clauses in the preliminary contract

  • A condition precedent with a minimum financing amount and an approval deadline.
  • Agree in writing what happens to the deposit if the financing condition is not met, with professional advice on the wording.
  • The price aligned with the expected valuation.
  • A deed date consistent with the validity of the bank’s approval.

Condominium and building rules

Read the rules on internal changes, short lets and pets. Check the works fund for approved extraordinary works. In buildings in the UNESCO area or the old centre there are extra restrictions on façades and windows.

A suggested sequence

  • First viewing and a price check against OMI values.
  • Ask the seller for the basic documents (energy certificate, floor plan, condominium).
  • Second viewing, with a technician if needed.
  • Indicative bank pre-approval.
  • Offer or proposal with conditions precedent.
  • Preliminary contract drafted by a notary or lawyer.

Sellers: prepare the pack

Providing the documents early saves time and strengthens the asking price.

Local points in Padova

Flood-prone areas and landscape restrictions in parts of the province call for hydraulic checks and historic authorisations. Restricted-traffic zones and parking affect appeal, not the legitimacy of the title: separate marketing from regulation.

Checklists from technical firms are a methodological inspiration only; every sale needs adapting and professional advice.
