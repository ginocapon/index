INTERNATIONAL JOURNAL · PROPERTY PLANNING

Buying property in Italy: before you sign

A practical first checklist for overseas buyers in Padova: your brief, independent advisers, documents, offer conditions and the next decision.

Prepared for Righetto International · Private editorial draft · Sources checked 24 November 2026

The first decision is not the property

Before making a purchase proposal, decide what the property needs to do for you. A permanent home, an occasional base and a rental investment create different questions. Write down who will use it, the intended use, the places you need to reach and the total capital you can commit. Keep requirements separate from preferences. A garden may be desirable; step-free access or permission for an intended business use may determine whether a property works at all.

Bring your own advisers in early

The Italian Notariat recommends contacting your chosen notary from the start of negotiations, before signing a purchase proposal or preliminary agreement. Those documents can already create binding commitments. The notary is an independent public official, not the estate agent’s representative. Ask what the notary will check, which technical checks need a separate qualified professional and what documents must be available before each stage.

Make the conditions explicit

Discuss the offer wording, any finance condition, checks, payment arrangements and deadlines with your advisers before signing. A reassuring verbal conversation is not a replacement for the written terms. Do not assume that a payment is refundable or that calling it a reservation makes it non-binding. Ask what happens if finance is refused, a document is missing or either party does not complete.

Overseas buyers need individual checks

Nationality and residence circumstances can affect the checks required. The Notariat explains reciprocity and lawful residence considerations for foreign buyers. Let the notary confirm your own position instead of relying on advice written for someone of another nationality or status. Raise language, interpretation, identification and any representation arrangements early enough for the professionals to plan the process.

A useful brief for Righetto

Introduce the intended use, preferred locality, approximate budget and any essential access requirements. If a listing interests you, include its reference and ask whether its price and availability are current. Righetto’s local starting point is Padova and its province. A first enquiry should not include identity documents, bank records or confidential client files. This checklist helps prepare a conversation; it does not approve a property or replace legal and technical advice.
