The confirmatory deposit (caparra confirmatoria) in Padova

Gino Capon · Adapted from the Italian article · Editorial revision 25 November 2026

What a confirmatory deposit does, how it differs from an advance payment, and the questions to settle before paying it.

In short

Under Civil Code article 1385, a confirmatory deposit has specific consequences if a party defaults. It is not a general right to change your mind. The amount and payment terms must be agreed for the transaction; this guide gives no standard market percentage.

What is a caparra confirmatoria?

Article 1385 concerns money or other fungible goods delivered by one party to the other as a confirmatory deposit when the contract is concluded. If the contract is performed, the deposit must be returned or credited towards the performance due. In a home sale, the parties should state clearly how it is counted towards the price.

Read the actual offer or preliminary contract before paying. The label, delivery of the money, agreed conditions and legal effect of the document all matter. Ask the notary or lawyer to review any ambiguity.

What happens if someone defaults?

If the party who gave the deposit defaults, article 1385 allows the other party to withdraw and retain it. If the recipient defaults, the non-defaulting party may withdraw and demand double the deposit.

The article also permits the non-defaulting party to seek performance or termination instead; damages then follow the general rules. These are different remedies, not an automatic right to combine every possible claim. Whether a default justifies a remedy depends on the facts and the contract. Take legal advice before giving notice or demanding money.

Do not confuse default with a lawful withdrawal right or with an agreed condition that has not been met. A dispute about financing, documents or responsibility cannot be decided merely by calling a payment a deposit.

How is it different from a payment on account?

An advance payment is part of the price. A confirmatory deposit has the additional function and remedies set out in article 1385. Do not infer the intended protection from an informal description or a payment reference alone.

Have the document identify the payment, its purpose, when it is due, who receives or holds it, the payment method and the conditions for its release or return. Keep the receipt and the signed documents.

How much should the deposit be?

Agree the amount in light of the transaction, available funds, financing conditions and the obligations each party is accepting. This article does not recommend a percentage or claim a typical Padova amount. Do not commit money you may need for other costs without checking the full budget and the consequences of default.

Discuss the payment timetable with the notary and your bank. A deposit is not the same thing as the whole part of the purchase price that the mortgage will not finance.

What if I need a mortgage?

Before signing, ask an adviser to draft a financing condition that reflects the intended amount, deadline and consequences if financing is not obtained. A bank valuation, a preliminary assessment and final lending approval are separate steps.

Do not assume a mortgage refusal automatically entitles you to recover the deposit. The outcome depends on the agreement, any condition and the circumstances. Conversely, do not assume every failed loan application automatically means the buyer has defaulted.

Registration and taxes

The Revenue Agency says a preliminary sale contract must be registered within 30 days of signing. Fixed registration tax is 200 euro, with stamp duty also due. If a notarial act is used, the notary handles registration.

For preliminary contracts signed from 1 January 2025, confirmatory deposits and non-VAT advances are subject to 0.5% registration tax, or the lower tax applicable to the final contract. The proportional tax is credited against the main registration tax due for the final contract.

VAT advances have different treatment. A confirmatory deposit is not itself consideration for a supply, so the Revenue Agency distinguishes it from a VAT advance. Ask the notary to calculate the actual taxes, including the fixed amounts and any applicable cap.

Do not assume the fixed registration tax is deducted at completion or that every tax is refunded if the sale fails. Ask a tax adviser about the treatment of sums retained or received after a default; that is separate from the contractual remedy.

Before you pay: a short checklist

  • Have the offer or preliminary contract reviewed, including its conditions and deadlines.
  • Confirm the property, parties and payment recipient.
  • Agree the amount, traceable payment method, custody and release terms.
  • Check financing and the documents needed before committing.
  • Ask who handles registration and whether transcription of the preliminary contract is appropriate.
  • Keep the signed contract, receipt and professional advice.

Questions buyers and sellers ask

Does the seller always return double?

No. Article 1385 gives that remedy to the non-defaulting party who withdraws because the recipient of the deposit has defaulted. It is not a rule for every cancellation or unmet condition.

Is a deposit percentage required here?

This guide gives no standard percentage. Agree the amount and terms with advice on the specific transaction.

Can the buyer simply change their mind and lose only the deposit?

Do not assume so. Article 1385 also addresses the alternative of seeking performance or termination under the general rules. Ask a lawyer about the obligations and available remedies before acting.
